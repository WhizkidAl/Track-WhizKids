Purpose

What are we trying to understand?

The key question is not simply how to increase compliance. It is what conditions allow the participant to remain regulated, communicate what they need, and participate meaningfully across the day, particularly in the afternoon.

1

Three-week whole-day participation & regulation tracker

Use a very brief whole-day picture to understand the afternoon pattern, not merely to prove afternoons are difficult.

The tracker now allows separate saves across the day: Mum can save the morning handover, and school can later save mid-morning, pre-lunch and end-of-day checkpoints independently.

Quick by default. Each school checkpoint only requires participation and regulation selections. If extra detail may help, staff can press Add context / strategies to record what was going on, what support was tried and whether it seemed to help. This optional detail includes factors such as noise/busy environment, schedule change, transition, crowding, difficult task, waiting/uncertainty, preferred activity ending, communication breakdown or sensory discomfort; and supports such as visual supports, First–Then/Next, choice, quiet/break space, movement/proprioceptive input, reduced demand, co-regulation, extra processing time, transition warning, environmental change or communication/AAC support.

Review after approximately three weeks should consider when participation changes, sleep/breakfast and medication patterns, activities/environments associated with better participation, which breaks genuinely help, whether proactive breaks are more effective than reactive breaks, and whether the participant is beginning to independently communicate a need for break, movement, quiet or help.

Meeting decision / action

2

Understand the significant incident

Reconstruct what happened rather than assuming a single cause.

Possible contributing factors may include cumulative fatigue, medication effect reducing, sensory load, language/comprehension demands, unexpected change, transitions, difficulty communicating a need, prolonged task demand, hunger/thirst, pain/discomfort, peer interaction, cumulative stress or a combination.

Incident reconstruction

3

Trial a different afternoon structure

Short activity → regulation/movement → short activity → purposeful choice → reset.

Afternoon demands may need to be deliberately different from morning demands. This does not mean removing learning; it means presenting learning and participation in a form that is accessible when regulatory capacity is lower.

Possible framework: Activity 1 (5–10 min) → body/regulation break → Activity 2 (5–10 min) → choice activity → quiet/reset → next activity.

What is feasible at school?

5

Quiet / low-stimulation space

Consider whether a genuinely quieter environment is available, particularly in the afternoon.

Possible options include a quiet library, outdoor area, garden, unused room, short walk with EA, or further modification of the existing classroom space. Sensory equipment should follow OT advice.

Meeting decision / action

6

Consolidate existing visual supports

Make supports consistent, portable, easy to manipulate and linked to a clear purpose.

Full daily schedule

A full-day schedule can remain available if useful, but does not need to be the primary support if it is visually dense or becomes a focus in itself.

First → Then → Next

Use a manageable immediate sequence and add Change and Choice cards so the participant can see a short sequence while also learning that plans can change and choices are possible.

Break choices

Break options should support the participant to identify a need, see available options, make a choice and access support before escalation. Breaks should not need to be earned.

7

Reward / preferred choices

Keep the distinction between a preferred activity following a task and a regulation break that must remain available when needed.

Important: A reward/preferred activity may follow completion of an agreed task. A regulation break must not have to be earned.
8

Morning routine visual

Keep it if it is genuinely being used and helpful; do not rebuild it automatically.

9

Activity sequence cards

Use preferred activities as opportunities for independence and communication.

Examples: CHOOSE → COLOUR → CUT → GLUE → FINISHED   or   CHOOSE → CUT → MAKE → SHOW.
10

Portable visual support kit

Once the team agrees what is useful, consolidate the core resources into one accessible system.

Possible core cards: First, Then, Next, Change, Choice, Break, Help, Quiet, Move, Finished and Something Else, plus preferred-activity, regulation-choice and activity-sequence cards. One reusable board can be configured for immediate sequence, break choices or activity choices.

11

Adult language & self-advocacy

Visuals should increasingly support the participant to communicate, not only function as adult instructions.

Adults should model and respond to: Break, Help, Quiet, Move, Change, No, Something else, My choice, Finished.

Initially an adult may identify the need: “Your body needs to move” and offer a small number of options. Over time, move toward “What does your body need?”
12

When the participant is already dysregulated

Reduce language and unnecessary demands, reduce environmental load, use familiar supports, prioritise safety/co-regulation and allow genuine recovery.

Do not immediately return to the full original task.
small achievable success → regulated/preferred activity → gradual return
13

Immediate resource build following the meeting

Only build the first consolidated set after school/EA/OT input clarifies what will actually be used.

Phase 1 resources

Three-week review

What will the team review?

The review should consider the whole-day pattern, whether the modified afternoon structure improves access, which activities work, which breaks genuinely regulate, whether quieter spaces help, whether the simplified visual system is actually used consistently, whether self-advocacy is increasing, and what should be kept, removed or modified.

Overall principle: the goal is not more visual information. It is the right amount of visual information at the right time: small immediate sequence + genuine choice + proactive regulation + self-advocacy + consistent adult response.
