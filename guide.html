How to use the tracker and shared action plan

The aim is to collect enough information to make better decisions without turning school or home into a data-entry exercise.

Participation trackerAfternoon action plan

The two tools do different jobs

Participation & regulation tracker

A short daily record used to look for patterns across sleep, breakfast, regulation, participation, context, support strategies and significant incidents.

Shared action plan

A collaborative planning document where family, school and clinicians can see the same information, refine it together, and agree what will be standardised or built next.

Daily tracker: the minimum useful entry

1
Morning context
Home can record sleep, breakfast, morning presentation and medication. A comment is optional.
2
Three school checkpoints
Mid-morning, just before lunch and end of day. Choose the closest participation and regulation options.
3
Add detail only when it helps
Keep the checkpoint quick. Only press “Add context / strategies” when extra detail would be useful; then record what was happening, what support was tried and whether it seemed to help. Free text remains optional.
More information can be useful, but it is not required every time. The tracker is meant to be quick enough to use consistently.

What the pattern view is looking for

Morning context

Does difficulty occur more often after poor sleep, reduced breakfast, an unsettled morning or a medication variation?

Time of day

Does difficulty cluster at a particular checkpoint rather than across the whole day?

Build-up before incidents

Was a significant incident preceded by regulation difficulty at two or three earlier checkpoints?

Context and support

Which context factors recur around difficulty, what strategies are being tried, and which are most often rated as helpful?

Pattern does not mean cause. The tool reports associations in the recorded data. Use the reflection section to add a working hypothesis, question or alternative explanation.

Reflections and working hypotheses

Anyone with authorised access can add a reflection. This is where the team can say, for example, “I wonder whether the transition into lunch is more relevant than breakfast” or “noise seems to be present on several difficult days.” These notes sit beside the data; they are not treated as proven findings.

Shared action plan: how to use it

1
Read before adding
Everyone sees the same document. Correct or refine existing content rather than starting a separate version.
2
Add what you know from your setting
For the afternoon action plan, family and school work in the same shared document to decide what afternoon structure, activities, spaces and supports are feasible. Clinicians add assessment and clinical reasoning where relevant.
3
Use the final sections for decisions
Record agreed actions, who will do them, what remains unresolved and what should be built next.
4
Save with your name/role
Each save creates a history snapshot so the team can see how the plan developed.

What to do if someone else is editing

If the plan has changed since you opened it, the page will stop you from saving over the newer version and ask you to reload first. This prevents one person from accidentally wiping out another person’s work.

Privacy and access

The website code is public, but participant data is not stored in GitHub. Participant data is stored in Supabase and is protected by sign-in plus row-level access rules. Only users who have been authorised for a participant can see that participant’s information.
